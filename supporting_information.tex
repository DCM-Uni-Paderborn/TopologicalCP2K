\section{Scope and Reproduction Boundaries}
This working document combines the retained band-topology calculations
transferred from the k-point manuscript with separately validated
transport, localizer, and quadratic-pseudospectrum developments.
The original results are not recomputed or relabelled by moving
the manuscript text.
The development status is recorded as of 25 September 2026.
A source freeze and complete public reproduction archive remain
submission requirements.

\subsection*{Applications versus test calculations}
TopoHSE-DB and the submitted generative--predictive discovery study
are cited in the main text as materials-science applications.
Their VASP-based calculations are not part of the CP2K validation
records collected here. In particular, their database labels and
candidate counts do not validate the new CP2K localizer or transport
implementations.

The smaller calculations below are tests and demonstrations.
The companion k-point paper additionally reports 18 converged Si/Al
calculations in conventional cubic cells with eight Si atoms
($a=5.43$~\AA) and four Al atoms ($a=4.05$~\AA).
These use PBE, DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH bases, GTH-PBE pseudopotentials,
600/60~Ry grid cutoffs, and an SCF threshold of $10^{-9}$.
Al uses Fermi--Dirac occupations at 1000~K, so its compared quantity
is the electronic free energy. The same-mesh comparison uses
$4\times4\times4$ sampling: 64 full-grid points, 32 with time reversal,
and four with either K290 or SPGLIB atomic symmetry.
The maximum cell-energy/free-energy difference is
$1.4\times10^{-12}$ hartree; the Al atomic-symmetry stress difference
is 0.098 bar. These tests demonstrate representation consistency,
not independently converged material properties or general force accuracy.
Their source and raw records remain those of the companion manuscript;
no new Si/Al calculations were performed for the present discussion.

\section{Native Wilson-Loop Test and Demonstration Calculations}

The topology examples form a separate calculation set from the Si/Al
mainline tests. The native extension supplies directed Gaussian-basis
overlaps, second-variational spinors, and the Wilson-loop analysis described
in the main text. The retained calculations were completed on
17 September 2026 with one MPI process and one OpenMP thread. Source
provenance and artifact checksums are recorded in
\texttt{validation/topology/README.md} and the figure manifest.

Both examples use PBE, the \texttt{DZVP-MOLOPT-GGA-GTH} basis from
\texttt{BASIS\_MOLOPT\_UZH}, SOC pseudopotentials from
\texttt{GTH\_SOC\_POTENTIALS}, a 200 Ry density cutoff and a 40 Ry relative
cutoff. The potential labels are \texttt{GTH-PBE-q8} for neon and
\texttt{GTH-PBE-q4} for tin. Complex orbitals and explicit full SCF meshes
are used. The neon reference contains one atom at fractional coordinate
$(1/2,1/2,1/2)$ in a cubic cell of side 5~\AA, with a $2\times2\times2$
SCF mesh and convergence threshold $10^{-9}$. The stanene cell has lattice
vectors $(4.674,0,0)$, $(2.337,4.047802737288466,0)$, and $(0,0,20)$~\AA,
and two tin atoms at fractional positions $(1/3,1/3,0.5213)$ and
$(2/3,2/3,0.4787)$. It therefore has a buckling height of 0.852~\AA.
These fixed geometric parameters follow the pristine-stanene reference
reported by Zhang et al.\footnote{R.-w. Zhang et al., Room temperature
quantum spin Hall insulator in ethynyl-derivative functionalized stanene
films, \emph{Sci. Rep.} \textbf{6}, 18879 (2016),
\url{https://doi.org/10.1038/srep18879}.}
An $8\times8\times1$ SCF mesh and convergence threshold $10^{-8}$ are used
for stanene. The examples test the topology implementation at these fixed
settings, rather than constitute a new structural or band-gap prediction.

Eight occupied spinors are retained in each case. The stanene
second-variational calculation includes all 26 scalar basis states, giving
52 spinor states before occupied-band selection. For neon, five scalar
states produce ten spinors. The selected half-plane is
$\mathbf k(s,t)=(t,s/2,0)$ in fractional reciprocal coordinates, with
$0\leq s,t\leq1$. The loop closes with reciprocal shift $(1,0,0)$.
Starting meshes contain four points on each of three loops for neon and
12 points on each of 13 loops for stanene. Refinement doubles the points
per loop and inserts a midpoint between neighboring transverse loops.
The accepted surfaces have $8\times5$ and $192\times193$ points,
respectively. The last center changes are $1.28\times10^{-5}$ and
$2.73\times10^{-4}$ in reduced units. The smallest link singular values
are 0.99826 and 0.95248.

The native invariants are zero for neon and one for stanene. Z2Pack 2.2.1
gives the same stanene parity using either the raw overlaps or their
unitary polar factors. For the latter, the maximum Wilson-eigenvalue
difference from the native calculation is $7.95\times10^{-14}$. This
comparison tests the loop algebra with identical input matrices. Separate
operator checks enforce zero-step normalization, reverse-link adjoint
relations, loop reversal, and atomic-position phases. A bonded two-atom
full-spinor check gives maximum adjoint and zero-step residuals of
$2.79\times10^{-14}$ and $2.00\times10^{-13}$, respectively.

An additional adaptive Z2Pack-driven stanene surface uses 42 CP2K line
calculations and ends with 15 accepted transverse lines, all satisfying
the line-position, movement, and gap checks, and again gives $\nu=1$.
This calculation chooses its sampling independently but uses the same
CP2K Hamiltonian and overlap implementation. The main-text figure shows
the native samples without interpolation or artificial separation of
degenerate centers. It is regenerated by
\texttt{python3 validation/topology/plot\_wilson.py}, which requires NumPy
and Matplotlib. Raw \texttt{.wilson} files, inputs, logs, comparison
reports, generated comma-separated figure data, and checksums accompany
the figure.

\subsection{Parameter-mesh discretization and physical closure}
Let $Q_\mu(x)$ be the polar-unitary selected-state overlap directed from
mesh vertex $x$ to $x+\hat\mu$. All boundary links include the specified
physical sewing. The oriented plaquette is
\begin{equation}
 P_{\mu\nu}(x)=Q_\mu(x)Q_\nu(x+\hat\mu)
 Q_\mu^\dagger(x+\hat\nu)Q_\nu^\dagger(x).
\end{equation}
With this link orientation, the two-parameter estimator is
\begin{equation}
 C_1^{\mathrm{mesh}}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_x
 \operatorname{Arg}\det P_{12}(x).
\end{equation}
Its sign matches the determinant Wilson-phase winding convention of the
main text. For four parameters, define the full principal matrix
logarithm $F_{\mu\nu}(x)=\log P_{\mu\nu}(x)$. Each $F_{\mu\nu}$ is
anti-Hermitian and already includes the plaquette area. The estimator is
\begin{equation}
 C_2^{\mathrm{mesh}}=-\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\sum_x
 \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}
 \left[F_{12}F_{34}-F_{13}F_{24}+F_{14}F_{23}\right](x).
 \label{eq:si-c2}
\end{equation}
The full matrices are needed for a non-Abelian occupied subspace.
Equation~\eqref{eq:si-c2} approximates the second Chern character pairing
and is not generally the second Chern class. Its finite-mesh value is
reported without integer rounding.

Physical links use $C_a^\dagger O_{ab}C_b$, with both spinor components
summed when present. The cross-geometry AO matrix $O_{ab}$ retains the
moving contracted Gaussian centers, periodic images, and reciprocal
boundary phases. The cell, atom count and selected rank remain fixed
throughout the family. Endpoint checks use explicit atom permutations,
lattice translations, basis matching, and selected-subspace sewing.
The code reports AO-metric errors, sampled selected/excluded-band gaps,
minimum link singular values, and principal plaquette phases. A refined
mesh must preserve the same physical family. Sampled isolation and
invariant convergence do not by themselves establish a bulk spectral
gap between samples. An indirect gap is interpreted only for a lowest-band
prefix with a justified common energy reference across geometries.

\section{Native Inversion Representations and Symmetry Signatures}
\label{sec:si-inversion}

The inversion extension was validated separately from the phason
extension and from the earlier Si/Al and Wilson calculations.
Its source patch, base snapshot, inputs and outputs remain in
\texttt{validation/topology/inversion-tqc/}.
These records identify the original tests without retrospectively
assigning them to another executable.

The \texttt{WANNIER90} print section accepts
\texttt{KPOINTS\_SOURCE TRIM}, \texttt{SOC T},
\texttt{TIME\_REVERSAL T} and \texttt{INVERSION\_TQC T}.
\texttt{TQC\_DIMENSION} selects the four $k_z=0$ TRIM or all eight
three-dimensional TRIM. \texttt{PARITY\_ORIGIN} specifies the inversion
center in fractional direct coordinates. The occupied spinor bands must
be a complete lowest-band prefix, with excluded states available for
checking spectral separation. A \texttt{PARITY}-only mode also accepts
isolated scalar subspaces. Neither route requires Z2Pack, Wannier90
execution or a Wannier fit. The TRIM calculation holds the converged
SCF potential fixed and does not require the SCF integration mesh to
contain those points. The two-dimensional option samples the fractional
$k_z=0$ plane of a three-dimensionally periodic cell, not a separate
two-dimensional Poisson boundary condition. The inversion center is
specified explicitly rather than inferred from a space-group search.

For an orbital of angular momentum $l$ on atom $a$, inversion about
$\mathbf c$ maps it to an identical Gaussian orbital on atom $b$ when
$2\mathbf c-\mathbf r_a=\mathbf r_b+\mathbf L$ with integer $\mathbf L$.
The coefficient multiplier is
$(-1)^l\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf L)$. The code verifies
atom-kind matching, involution, metric covariance, $C^\dagger SC=I$,
unitarity and Hermiticity of the projected inversion matrix, and its
commutator with the selected eigenvalues. Spinful time reversal is
checked through the sewing matrix of $\Theta=\mathrm{i}\sigma_y K$.
Parity multiplicities must be even before division into Kramers pairs.
The dimensionless and geometry tolerances are $10^{-6}$ and
$10^{-6}$ bohr, respectively. The energy-commutator tolerance is
$10^{-6}$ hartree.

For $N$ occupied Kramers pairs and odd counts $n^-_\kappa$ at
$\mathbf k=\kappa/2$, $\kappa\in\{0,1\}^d$, the parity index is
$\nu=\sum_\kappa n^-_\kappa\bmod2$. In three dimensions the weak indices
restrict this sum to $\kappa_i=1$, and the convention for the inversion
indicator is $z_4=\sum_\kappa n^-_\kappa\bmod4$.
The analytic inversion-subgroup EBRs are local even or odd Kramers pairs
at centers $\mathbf c+\mathbf a/2$. Define
\begin{equation}
 d_{\mathbf a}=2^{-d}\sum_\kappa(-1)^{\mathbf a\cdot\kappa}
                     (N-2n^-_\kappa).
\end{equation}
An integer atomic-signature decomposition exists when all
$d_{\mathbf a}$ are integers and $N-\sum_{\mathbf a}d_{\mathbf a}$ is even.
A nonnegative decomposition additionally requires
$N\geq\sum_{\mathbf a}|d_{\mathbf a}|$. The implementation makes these
decisions with integer arithmetic. Atomic compatibility concerns the
symmetry signature, not a proof of trivial topology. Likewise, a
signed-only decomposition does not by itself establish fragile topology.
This inversion-only EBR test is distinct from the subsequent native
little-group and explicit-segment analysis described below; it does
not become a general EBR classifier when that additional analysis is used.

\section{Native Little-Group and Compatibility Validation}
\label{sec:si-little-groups}
The native algebra constructs unitary projective character tables,
Wigner corepresentation restrictions, and endpoint-to-segment
branching without a runtime dependency on Spgrep or a pretabulated
irrep catalogue. The electronic-structure path uses SPGLIB to detect
spatial operations and, when requested, adds their nonmagnetic
time-reversal extension. It retains centering translations and tests
the metric, band-subspace closure, energy commutators and the complete
semilinear multiplication law before accepting band multiplicities.

The all-setting sweep covers all 530 Hall settings of the 230 ordinary
space groups. At each setting, it samples
$(0,0,0)$, $(1/2,1/2,1/2)$, $(1/2,0,0)$, $(1/3,1/3,0)$,
$(0,0,1/4)$ and $(0.173,0.217,0.319)$ with scalar and spinful factors.
The invariant cell metric is obtained by averaging over the supplied
rotations. Both the ordinary group and its grey extension are checked.
Generic-site orbits with physical spin multiplicity provide fixed-rank
compatible band data. Segments run from Gamma to the other sampled
points and from Gamma to its reciprocal image $(0,0,1)$.
This tests directed-image monodromy as well as ordinary endpoint
restrictions; it does not sample every possible line or material.

Analytic cases separately test magnetic half translations, spin

The Gaussian directory uses UZH MOLOPT bases for its GPW fixtures and
a contracted all-electron basis for GAPW. Ne tests exercise scalar
and post-SCF GTH-SOC multiplets at Gamma, a generic PT-symmetric

single screw branch must report its monodromy obstruction.
The generated Wilson-link test also exercises repeated refinement.
Serial runs use two OpenMP threads; MPI runs use two ranks with two
threads each and the thread-safe BLAS settings stated below.
An earlier wider corepresentation integration pass additionally gave
110/110 serial and 111/111 MPI assertions across topology, Kubo,
localizer and quadratic directories. These are separate test scopes,
not numbers to add into a material benchmark total.

A separate local algebra sweep uses SPGLIB 2.7 magnetic data in the
supplied setting for every one of the 1,651 magnetic UNI numbers.
At the same six points and two spin choices, all 19,812 factor cases
pass; 14,088 contain an antiunitary stabilizer, and 5,724 do not.
For 10,154 primitive-cell tables supported by an independent
Spgrep 0.7.0 comparison, dimensions, Wigner types and unitary
restriction characters agree within $7.11\times10^{-15}$.
Both calculations use the same supplied factor system, so this is
not an independent validation of symmetry detection. Moreover, a
separate semilinear-product check did not validate every explicit
antiunitary matrix produced by that reference calculation.
Those matrices are not imported into CP2K; the comparison is limited
to types, dimensions and unitary restrictions. CP2K's actual Gaussian
sewing matrices pass their own full product check.

The separate native site-induction test samples fractional positions
$(0,0,0)$, $(1/2,0,1/4)$ and $(0.173,0.217,0.319)$ at the same six
reciprocal points. Ordinary and grey groups, each with scalar and
spinful factors, give 38,160 site/k-point cases over all 530 Hall
settings. There are 67,824 local-representation columns evaluated and
decomposed, and the same number of directed compatibility comparisons.
Their atomic band rank equals the orbit multiplicity times the local
irrep or corepresentation dimension. This construction preserves
conventional-cell translation sectors rather than silently converting
them to a primitive-cell catalogue.

An independent check constructs Cartesian s/p/d orbital traces and,
for spinors, multiplies them by the physical SU(2) trace. After matching
the onsite orbital content, its explicitly translated orbit trace
agrees with the corresponding induced character sum in 114,480
comparisons. The numerical acceptance tolerance is $10^{-7}$ for
these summed traces and decomposition checks; group and orbit geometry
use $10^{-9}$. Analytic checks additionally cover inversion-center
parities, screw orbits, spin-quaternion doublets, and complex local
orbitals related by an antiunitary half translation. These are
algorithmic reference tests, not new material calculations or a new
production input mode.
Additional scalar and spinful checks preserve the characters under
passive origin shifts and unimodular changes to a sheared cell, and
verify the expected Bloch rephasing under integer changes of operation
representatives. The full database sweeps pass in serial, two-rank MPI,
and a separate bounds-checked build with floating-point traps.

The native Wyckoff generator separately constructs 3,467 affine
site-symmetry families across all 530 Hall settings and determines
5,648 directed specialization relations. External validation uses
SPGLIB's tabulated \texttt{Wyckoff.csv} coordinates, not the production
fixed-set algorithm, to check dimensions, orbit multiplicities,
family equivalence and closure containment. Every family matches
bijectively, with largest affine residual $2.22\times10^{-16}$.
The reference table's version and checksum, comparison script and
results are retained in the development record. No tabulated
coordinates are read by the CP2K implementation.

For each generated seed the separate spinful induction kernel
confirms its orbit and site stabilizer. Combined arbitrary origin
shifts, nonorthogonal unimodular cell changes, integer shifts of
operation representatives and reordered operations preserve the
families and their specialization graph. Analytic tests cover
inversion centers, intersecting mirrors, fixed-point-free screw
axes, grey groups and an antiunitary half translation. Failure to
resolve a generic stabilizer or an arithmetic/work limit returns
no partial list. Maximal site groups are identified geometrically;
this is not an elementarity test for their induced representations.
The complete Wyckoff sweep passes in serial release, two-rank MPI
and bounds-checked debug builds.

The subsequent atomic-signature module combines all generated sites
and onsite irreps/corepresentations over the six reciprocal points
listed above. Ordinary/grey and scalar/spinful variants yield 2,120
matrices, 31,079 provenance-preserving columns and 707,038 integer
entries. Seven directed comparisons per column include the supplied
point connections and a reciprocal-image loop, totalling 217,553
column/segment checks. Each block reproduces the column's band rank;
antiunitary rows count full corepresentations rather than treating all
states as Kramers pairs. Additivity, permuted operation rows, and
integer coset rephasing are checked. All sixteen inversion-center
signatures and the generic-site signature are recovered analytically.

These full sweeps pass in serial release, two-rank MPI and debug with
bounds and floating-point checks. A local optimized-compiler issue in automatic
\texttt{MATMUL} result resizing was independently reproduced without
CP2K and avoided by explicitly dimensioning the test vectors; no
physical reference values were adjusted. The compiler reproducer and
validation logs are retained separately from material results.
No new general-classification input mode, elementarity assertion or
complete reciprocal-stratum coverage follows from these matrix tests.

The integer-signature extension factors these same 2,120 matrices
using checked 64-bit Smith reduction. Each factorization verifies its
full integer certificate. One signed target per matrix is reconstructed,
and the nonnegative search recovers every atomic column and a
three-column sum per matrix: 33,199 witnesses in total. These deliberately
constructed targets test recovery, not a classification of DFT bands.

An independent unit program compares Smith invariants with the gcds
of all minors and computes the determinants of both unimodular
transforms for 384 matrices with dimensions one through four.
The rank-zero through rank-four case counts are 38, 167, 103, 60 and 16.
The analytic inversion Fourier criterion supplies a separate exact
oracle for every odd-parity-count pattern with two Kramers pairs:
among 81 four-TRIM patterns, 41 admit signed and 33 admit nonnegative
atomic signatures; among 6,561 eight-TRIM patterns, the counts are
241 and 129. Thus signed-only cases are tested explicitly.
For every $2\times3$ matrix with entries in $\{0,1,2\}$ and every
two-component target in $\{0,\ldots,4\}^2$, a brute-force enumeration
of coefficients zero through four supplies another oracle, covering
18,225 systems. Coefficients beyond four cannot contribute to a
target in this range through any nonzero nonnegative column.

\subsection{Character functions beyond finite reciprocal samples}
Equal characters at selected reciprocal points need not imply equal
character functions. We distinguish these cases without replacing
the sampled integer signature matrix or its real-space induction
witnesses. For a unitary operation with integer direct-space rotation
$W$, the invariant reciprocal torus is
\begin{equation}
 K_W=\{\mathbf k\bmod\mathbb Z^3:
          (W^T-I)\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z^3\}.
\end{equation}
Its character lattice is $\mathbb Z^3/(W-I)\mathbb Z^3$.
Indeed, two integer shifts differing by $(W-I)\mathbf n$ give
identical Bloch phases on $K_W$. For the checked Smith factorization
$U(W-I)V=D$, each finite induction term
$c_j\exp(-2\pi i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf L_j)$ is therefore collected
under the key $U\mathbf L_j$, modulo each nonzero Smith divisor.
Rows with zero divisor retain an unrestricted integer key.
The coefficients $c_j$ include the local character, the spin-lift
factor and, where required, conjugation by an antiunitary orbit
representative. Antiunitary operators themselves have no
basis-independent trace and are not assigned character functions.

These quotient characters are linearly independent on the complete
compact group $K_W$. Equality of the collected coefficients thus
tests equality of the finite character functions, including all
disconnected torsion components, rather than fitting more k points.
Integer operations are checked for overflow; complex coefficients
are compared at $10^{-9}$ after scaling by band dimension in this
audit. The production diagnostic uses the supplied character
tolerance and rejects nontransitive tolerance neighborhoods.
It reports local unitary-character classes but does not merge
atomic columns, change membership decisions or infer an intertwiner
between Bloch bundles.

As an analytic counterexample to Gamma-only matching, equal-parity
orbitals at inversion centers $(0,0,0)$ and $(1/2,0,0)$ have the
same Gamma characters but different characters on other components
of the inversion-fixed torus. The Fourier comparison distinguishes
both parity pairs. With only Gamma supplied to the atomic catalogue,
all sixteen centered inversion columns and the generic-orbit column
remain distinct, also with Kramers pairing.

For the 378 multi-candidate references in the preceding
230-space-group EBR audit, however, every candidate set has equal
collected unitary-character coefficients in both tested coordinate
conventions. Optimized serial and both MPI processes agree.
These ambiguities are therefore not removed by denser sampling;
canonical onsite conventions and bundle-level information remain
separate requirements. Independent evaluation of the collected
Fourier sums against raw Bloch induction checks 213,238 operation/
reciprocal-point queries per optimized executable or MPI process,

$4.1\times10^{-15}$. This count includes the bounded native

$E/t=-2\cos k+0.031$ and $\kappa_T/t=1.3$,
the lowest quadratic eigenvalue is $(0.031t)^2$.
The numerical state has $\langle T\rangle=e^{ik}$ and a
vanishing translation residual within $10^{-12}$.
This checks the translation phase itself, not only its real part.

A second periodic fixture has six three-site cells, hopping
amplitudes $-0.8t,-0.8t,-1.2t$ and
$\kappa_T/t=0.7$. For each supercell phase
$q=2\pi m/6$, $m=0,\ldots,5$, an independent three-dimensional
Bloch block sets $T_{12}=T_{23}=1$, $T_{31}=e^{iq}$ and
$H_{12}=H_{23}=-0.8t$, $H_{31}=-1.2te^{iq}$, with Hermitian
completion of $H$. The five energies
$E/t=-2.2,-1,0,0.7,2$ and 13 momenta
$k=-\pi+2\pi j/13$, $j=0,\ldots,12$, define 65 queries.
The complete 18-dimensional spectrum at every query is
compared with the sorted union of all six Bloch-block spectra.
All 1,170 eigenvalues agree within $1.60\times10^{-14}t^2$.
This comparison checks the cell-folding construction rather
than selected minima of an unfolding plot.

For the open-chain test, the wraparound translation is removed,
the trimerized hoppings are retained only between neighboring
sites, and site 4 has onsite energy $0.17t$.
The query is $E/t=0.33$, $z=e^{0.73i}$ and
$\kappa_T/t=1.3$. A separately assembled normal-product
reference uses LU metric solves and a generalized Hermitian
eigensolver. Its full spectrum agrees with the quadratic solver
within $3.56\times10^{-15}t^2$. Writing $T=C+iD$ and instead
adding the two Hermitian residual squares drops $i[C,D]$;
the largest ordered-eigenvalue discrepancy is
$8.604947\times10^{-2}t^2$. This is a deliberate negative control,
not numerical noise or an adjustable reference value.

A nonsingular complex upper-bidiagonal basis change sets
$F_{\ell\ell}=1+0.02\ell$ for $\ell<18$, $F_{18,18}=1$, and
$F_{\ell,\ell+1}=0.21-0.12\mathrm{i}$. In the transformed problem,
$S=F^\dagger F$ and $A'=F^\dagger A F$.
Spectra change by at most $5.33\times10^{-15}t^2$ and
complex expectations, expressed in model units, by
$2.25\times10^{-14}$. Complex shifts of each operator and its
query leave the residual spectrum invariant. Restarted iteration
with ten trial vectors obtains the two lowest states; their
projector agrees with the dense reference within $10^{-8}$
in maximum element norm. The test compares the subspace,
not arbitrary individual eigenvector phases.

The distributed fixture uses six AO blocks of size three,
a complex nonorthogonal metric and a nonnormal complex
translation-like operator. Serial and one-, two- and four-rank
MPI runs cover both row- and column-oriented process grids.
Forward and adjoint products against dense references differ
by at most $4.45\times10^{-16}$ in the scaled model units;
the largest metric-solve residual is $4.58\times10^{-16}$.
Dense-factor and MUMPS-factor iterations agree with the two
reference eigenvalues within $8.78\times10^{-15}t^2$, while
their eigenpair residuals reach $9.84\times10^{-11}t^2$.
Supplying the forward operator in place of its adjoint is
deliberately tested and rejected. These tiny MPI fixtures
check communication and conjugation, not speedup or memory scaling.

Focused instrumented tests also pass bounds checking,
undefined-behavior checks and floating-point traps in the
numerical kernel. Traps are temporarily disabled only around

the affected quadratic/localizer directories pass 53/53 serial

The model tests above do not, by themselves, validate translated
Gaussian integrals or the continuum projection complement. The

\subsection{Gaussian translations and continuum projection leakage}
\label{sec:si-gaussian-translation}
The \texttt{FORMULATION TRANSLATION} adapter evaluates
$T_{\mu\nu}(\bm a)=\langle\phi_\mu|U(\bm a)|\phi_\nu\rangle$
with $U(\bm a)\psi(\bm r)=\psi(\bm r+\bm a)$.
To test this independently of its pair assembly, a second CP2K
calculation adds ghost atoms with the same bases at
$\bm R_B-\bm a$. The cross block of this enlarged system's
ordinary overlap matrix supplies a separate $T$ reference.
Printed $H$ and $S$, this ghost-overlap block, and the exported
complex quadratic states determine an independent generalized
normal Gram and all observable checks. The reference uses NumPy
metric solves and Hermitian diagonalization, not the new adapter.
It shares CP2K's existing Gaussian-integral routines, so a separate
analytic primitive-Gaussian case also checks their convention.

The four fixtures use a 7~\AA{} isolated box, PBE, a 150~Ry
cutoff, 30~Ry relative cutoff, $\epsilon_{\rm SCF}=10^{-10}$,
and $\epsilon_{\rm default}=10^{-12}$. Single atoms are at
$(3,3,3)$~\AA; H$_2$ has its second atom at
$(3.74,3,3)$~\AA. The query is
$E=-0.1$ hartree, $\kappa_T=0.3$ hartree,
$\bm a=(0.63,-0.37,0.21)$~\AA{} and
$\bm k=(0.31,-0.22,0.18)$ bohr$^{-1}$, with no extra $2\pi$

$q=8$ for Ne. Ne uses GAPW, the others GPW.
One quadratic state is requested for the single Gaussian and
three for the other cases. Iteration uses up to 14 trial vectors,
2,000 iterations and a $10^{-10}$ relative eigenpair-residual

\subsection{Periodized translations and independent Gamma supercells}
\label{sec:si-torus-translation}
The periodic translation construction uses the full
Born--von--Karman AO space. For cell-copy indices $i,j$ and
torus lattice matrix $h_N=h\operatorname{diag}(N_1,N_2,N_3)$,
the block element is the sum of ordinary Gaussian overlaps with
ket displacement
$\bm R_B+\bm R_j-\bm R_A-\bm R_i+h_N\bm n-\bm a$.
Only periodic axes are summed. Reciprocal-plane distances bound
all potentially overlapping images, also in a skew cell.
The full continuum normal Gram and leakage decomposition from
the previous subsection remain applicable. The Hamiltonian and
overlap use the same frozen-potential full-cell image lifting as
the periodic localizer; no irreducible-SCF weights enter.

The independent reference again obtains $T$ from ordinary
overlap matrices of a separate shifted ghost-basis calculation.
Here it uses a three-cell Gamma supercell, whereas the primitive
calculation has a Gamma-centered $3\times1\times1$ mesh. Both
SCFs are converged independently. The original Gamma Hamiltonian
and overlap, not the ghost-system Hamiltonian, enter the NumPy

\subsection{Silicon Bloch-band reference and integral screening}
\label{sec:si-silicon-translation}
A bonded-crystal control uses diamond Si with conventional
lattice constant $a=5.43$~\AA. The primitive lattice vectors are
the columns of
$h=(a/2)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&1\\1&0&1\\1&1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
and the two sites are $(a/8,a/8,a/8)$ and
$(3a/8,3a/8,3a/8)$. PBE/GPW calculations use
TZVP-MOLOPT-GGA-GTH-q4 and GTH-PBE-q4 from the UZH files,

\texttt{EPS\_SCF}$=10^{-10}$ and no smearing.
The SCF mesh is Gamma-centered $3\times1\times1$, without
symmetry reduction, and the complex full-AO analysis torus has

cell choices are an algebraic validation, not a converged Si

For $\bm a=h\bm e_1$, the exact translation permutes cell copies.

 \lambda_n(q;E,k)=(\epsilon_n(q)-E)^2

labels do not add Bloch states to the three-point analysis mesh.

\paragraph{Displaced Si and a noncommuting translation.}
Starting from the six-atom Gamma supercell above, only the second
atom is moved by $0.1$~\AA\ along the Cartesian $x$ direction.

is disabled because some ghost centers coincide with real centers;
the ghost calculation is used only for overlap integrals, not
for its electronic state.

 Q=(H-ES)S^{-1}(H-ES)
       +\kappa_T^2(2S-z^*T-zT^\dagger).
\end{equation}
The independently diagonalized lowest 105 eigenvalues agree
with the native two-rank iterative results within
$3.73\times10^{-14}$ hartree$^2$; the largest independently

complex translation means agree within $6.0\times10^{-14}$,
and squared leakage norms within $8.8\times10^{-14}$.

compressed translation have a commutator norm of
$0.3512$ hartree, and
$\|S^{-1/2}(S-T^\dagger S^{-1}T)S^{-1/2}\|_2=0.14677$.

$0.003649$ to $0.068246$ and the largest gap change relative to
the pristine cell is $0.013254$ hartree. These nonzero values
test the continuum projection complement and sensitivity to the
distortion. They are not converged defect spectral weights or
a topological classification.

\subsection{Stanene: a nontrivial band reference and finite-torus limits}
\label{sec:si-stanene-localizer}
Fresh calculations on 25 September use the two-atom stanene geometry
of Section~S2, but explicitly set both cell and Poisson periodicity
to XY, with the analytic two-dimensional Poisson solver and 20 \AA\
of nonperiodic cell height. This is a new boundary-condition control,
not a relabelling of the earlier three-dimensionally repeated slab.
The restricted PBE SCF uses a full $8\times8\times1$ Monkhorst--Pack
mesh, complex wavefunctions, threshold $10^{-9}$, and post-SCF
GTH-PBE-q4 SOC. Two numerical settings are compared:
DZVP-MOLOPT-GGA-GTH-q4 with 200 Ry and
TZVP-MOLOPT-GGA-GTH-q4 with 400 Ry, both from
\texttt{BASIS\_MOLOPT\_UZH}, with relative cutoff 40 Ry.
All 26 or 34 scalar AOs are retained for the SOC diagonalization.
Changing basis and cutoff together is a strengthened control,
not separate extrapolations in either parameter.

For the eight occupied spinors, both Wilson surfaces converge to
$\nu=1$ on 193 loops with 192 points per loop. The sampled indirect

$(-4.40591061,-4.32934210)$ eV. The smallest link singular values

the circular WCC differences from the native spectra are below
$10^{-13}$. Agreement on these identical matrices checks loop
algebra, not an independent density-functional approximation or
proof against an unsampled band crossing.

\subsection{Complete-Bloch flattening and larger sparse torus controls}
\label{sec:si-stanene-large-torus}
The periodic native flattening step now exploits exact translation
covariance. Complete primitive Bloch eigensystems replace the
whole-torus sign iteration, while the final native localizer
remains in its distributed real-cell basis. Seven constructed
complex pencils cover three mesh directions, one- and two-spin
layouts, unequal atomic block sizes, a single-cell limit and
internal degeneracy. They agree with exact matrices and the
previous whole-torus route within $3.4\times10^{-15}$.
Additional cases reject a broken translation, a gap closing, an
indefinite overlap and a globally ill-conditioned metric even
when individual Bloch metrics are well conditioned.
Serial and two-/four-rank tests include different process grids.
The native $N=3$ and $N=6$ stanene comparisons retain the
previous independent indices, with gap differences below
$6.9\times10^{-13}$ hartree. The original evidence in
Sec.~\ref{sec:si-native-flattening} remains separately archived.

Profiling the larger native dense calculation also identifies a
removable cubic-cost basis conversion. The signed permutation
$C$ permits the exact identity
$Q^\dagger LQ=(L+CLC+i(CL-LC))/2$. Evaluating its four entries
per output element removes the dense $Q$, an additional dense
complex work array and their matrix multiplications. Thirteen
constructed skew matrices of orders 4 through 52 reproduce both
the explicit dense-unitary formula and the original matrices
within $1.4\times10^{-15}$. Broken AII structure and invalid
dimensions are rejected. This optimization does not change the
physical time-reversal convention, factorization or gap test.
The completed two-rank native DZVP $N=8$ calculation reproduces
all three independent full-band queries within
$5.8\times10^{-14}$ hartree: at $\eta/\Delta=0.75,1,1.25$,

not removed by the optimized conversion.

\subsubsection{Independent pivot-correction control}
A subsequent dependency-level test identifies an integer overload
of the unqualified absolute-value function in the compiled
skew-LDL pivot search. Subunit floating-point pivot candidates
were therefore compared after truncation to integers. Replacing
this call by the scalar type's magnitude function leaves the
matching, candidate rows, pivot threshold, operator entries and
$10^{-10}$ solve-residual criterion unchanged.
An $8\times8$ skew matrix with integer entries and exactly
$\operatorname{Pf}(A)=60$ supplies an independent regression:
the affected library fails all six combinations of two
permutations and scales $1,10^{-120},10^{120}$, whereas the
corrected library gives the expected signs with residuals below
$2\times10^{-15}$. The exact value follows from recursive
Pfaffian expansion, not from the corrected factorization.
The small stanene $N=3$, $\eta/\Delta=0.5$ case then gives

dense reference. Local singular elimination blocks can nevertheless
require pivot partners outside the current supernode; this
correction does not implement such delayed pivots.

TZVP $N=12$, $\eta/\Delta=0.75$: with the process-local

both resolve $\nu=1$, with residuals $8.02\times10^{-12}$ and

remaining factorization limitation, not a changed physical
operator or evidence for a topology transition. No tolerance is
relaxed and no failed query is replaced by a reference integer.

\subsubsection{Independent right-hand sides and symbolic-retry research}
Probing a factorization only with $b=Ax$ restricts its test
right-hand sides to the range of $A$ and can therefore conceal
nullspace errors. The native adapter now solves four deterministic
right-hand sides independent of $A$ and retains the same
$10^{-10}$ relative-residual threshold. A rank-22 skew matrix of
order 24, constructed as $LDL^T$ with unit lower-triangular $L$
and one zero $2\times2$ block in $D$, supplies an analytic
regression without zero matrix rows. Its entries are dyadic
rationals, so the unscaled construction has exact nullity two.
Odd permutations and scales $1,10^{-120},10^{120}$ must all be
rejected. The analytic rank is used here rather than the sign
of a roundoff-sized last pivot in dense elimination.

A separate deterministic set contains 600 size/scale/permutation
queries at orders 8--96, including 132 numerically singular
queries. The former range-restricted probes accept 72 of those

\subsubsection{Delayed skew pivots without symbolic restarts}
A subsequent, separate numerical extension passes rejected pivot variables
and their Schur contributions to the parent front of the Tacho elimination
tree. Original matrix entries are assembled exactly once, at the front
owning their earlier symbolic endpoint. A child update contains both its
separator and any delayed variables. A candidate pivot is tested against
both complete updated columns, including the separator, with relative
threshold 0.01 and the unchanged absolute cutoff on the normalized matrix.
At the root a rook search replaces further delays. No matrix entries are
dropped and no pivot perturbation or reference-sign substitution is used.

For an accepted skew block
$D_j=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&d_j\\-d_j&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
the multipliers are $L_{21}=A_{21}D_j^{-1}$ and the trailing update is
$A_{22}-L_{21}D_jL_{21}^T$.
A blocked implementation accumulates 64 accepted columns before applying
this update with BLAS level 3. The sign is the product of the signs of
the upper-block entries $d_j$, multiplied by the parity of the complete
elimination permutation. Stored panels also provide forward, block-diagonal
and backward solves for the four independent right-hand sides.

At this prototype checkpoint the implementation is an uninstalled,
serial skew-specific extension,
not a change to the general indefinite solver or a distributed Pfaffian
factorization. Its final research adapter uses the public Tacho
\texttt{GraphTools\_Metis} and \texttt{SymbolicTools} interfaces;
private driver accessors were used only in preceding experiments.
For accepted test components of order at most 512, an additional dense
check reconstructs $LDL^T$ in the actual elimination order. This checks
the factors; it is not a dense fallback for the index.

The 600-query synthetic suite now resolves all 468 nonsingular queries
and rejects all 132 singular queries. An additional deterministic set
contains 636 queries, with orders 2--258, four random permutations per
matrix, three overall scales, banded, separator, dense and disconnected
patterns, and analytically singular dyadic congruences. All 336 nonsingular
queries are resolved correctly and all 300 singular queries are rejected.
These tests run with undefined-behaviour instrumentation of the adapter
and new numerical extension and C++ standard-library assertions; no
instrumentation error is reported. The separately compiled dependencies
are not covered by that instrumentation.
Across these two sets, 834 accepted nontrivial component factors are
explicitly reconstructed, with maximum relative Frobenius residual
$1.04\times10^{-13}$. Rebuilding the adapter from its standalone CMake
entry point and replaying both sets reproduces the same signs and
singular rejections.

\subsubsection{Native integration and structural-roundoff controls}
The delayed-skew numerical extension is subsequently integrated into
CP2K's existing narrow C interface, using the public Tacho symbolic
analysis and BLAS interfaces. It does not copy the dependency's
fixed-front numerical implementation or introduce a general-purpose
indefinite solver. The input keyword \texttt{PFAFFIAN\_MEMORY}
limits requested double-precision factor, pivot, frontal, Schur-update
and solve buffers in MiB, with default 4096. The matching, sparse input,
symbolic ordering, integer metadata, allocator overhead, DBCSR and
MUMPS are outside this limit. The reported peak covers only these
numerical buffers, not whole-process memory. Scalar panel counts are
reported separately. Disconnected components are processed sequentially,
so their peaks form a maximum while their panel counts are summed.
Tests exercise an exactly sufficient budget, a one-byte reduction,
collective exhaustion and inconsistent positive limits across MPI ranks.

The first complete integration run exposed a Bismuth SOC failure that
the exactly skew model matrices did not reveal. Tolerated floating-point
roundoff can give a nonzero entry on only one side of the skew matrix;
passing that asymmetric graph to symbolic analysis can omit a required
frontal entry. The native implementation consistently uses
\[
 A_{\mathrm{s}}=\tfrac12\bigl(\operatorname{Re} A-
                         (\operatorname{Re} A)^T\bigr)
\]
and the symmetric union of the sparse patterns, without an entry-drop
threshold. The discarded part $E=A-A_{\mathrm{s}}$, including its
imaginary part, obeys
\[
 \|E\|_2\leq\sqrt{\|E\|_1\|E\|_\infty}
       \leq\max(\|E\|_1,\|E\|_\infty).
\]
This computed upper bound must pass both the structural tolerance and
the existing gap-relative bound in the positive AO metric. It is not
legitimate to restore physical time reversal when this test fails.
A 32-site skew-chain reproducer tests all 930 possible one-sided
off-pattern couplings of magnitude $10^{-15}$. Before correction,
435 placements fail; afterwards all signs agree with the exact chain
Pfaffian. Transposed, odd-permuted and extreme-scale variants enter the
native unit test, and larger skew defects remain rejected.
The Bismuth SOC gap is $0.0681101299687$ hartree, consistent with
the unchanged dense reference, with solve residual
$1.40\times10^{-15}$.

\subsection{A continuous finite-model scale window}
The index need not remain constant for arbitrarily small or large
$\kappa$. At the centroid and each calculation's own neutral gap
midpoint, a finer scan identifies a common nontrivial window near
$0.003$ hartree/bohr. To check the unsampled intervals, write
\begin{equation}
 L(\kappa)=L(0)+\kappa D,\qquad
 \|D\|=\|(X-xI)-i(Y-yI)\|=19.89764\ {\rm bohr}.
\end{equation}
Here $X,Y$ are the complete, metric-orthonormalized finite AO
position matrices. The smallest absolute eigenvalue obeys
$|g(\kappa)-g(\kappa')|\leq\|D\||\kappa-\kappa'|$.
For a subinterval $[a,b]$ with midpoint $m$, the implementation
therefore uses the lower bound
\begin{equation}
 g(\kappa)\geq g(m)-\|D\|(b-a)/2-\epsilon_{\rm num},
 \qquad \epsilon_{\rm num}=10^{-10}\ {\rm hartree}.
\end{equation}
Recursive bisection covers
$[0.0025,0.0035]$ hartree/bohr with 11 accepted subintervals
and 21 midpoint evaluations for each temperature. The minimum
covered bounds are $9.10\times10^{-5}$ hartree at 300 K and
$7.95\times10^{-5}$ hartree at 30 K. Since the anchor at
$\kappa=0.003$ has $\nu=1$, neither finite model can change
its index within this numerically resolved interval. The explicit
roundoff margin is not an interval-arithmetic proof, and the
conclusion applies at the specified energy and position, not
simultaneously to all sampled queries or to the infinite material.

The original methods, native outputs, spectra and moments, neutral
scans, factorization comparison and interval bounds are retained under
\path{validation/development/} in the
\path{bismuth-neutral-validation/} directory, together with links
to the earlier 300 K evidence. Archive-only replay of
the 30 K scan independently checks all 45 queries and frontier
weights. Analytic tests include invariant degenerate-subspace
weights, gap-cover tiling and rejection of unresolved or nonfinite
gaps. These controls are numerical validation, not substitutes for
physical convergence of the flake.

and neutral midpoint, the 16-atom localizer instead changes its

The attempted gap cover over $[0.0025,0.0035]$ therefore correctly
fails: it retains an unresolved region near
$0.0026215985$ hartree/bohr after 249 midpoint evaluations.
This negative result is archived, not replaced by a successful
narrower scan. Varying $\kappa$ changes the localizer, not the
fixed electronic Hamiltonian or its electronic gap.

For $[0.00275,0.0035]$ hartree/bohr, 13 midpoint evaluations
cover seven subintervals with $\nu=1$, a norm constant of
15.22720 bohr and a minimum numerical lower bound of
$4.64539\times10^{-5}$ hartree. This interval is also contained
in the resolved intervals of both 18-atom temperature controls.
Thus all three finite Hamiltonians have a common central scale
window, although this does not remove their geometry and basis
dependence or establish a thermodynamic limit.

A five-step native restart evaluates the same 45 queries and
exports a fresh complete spectrum for independent reconstruction.
All 45 indices agree, with maximum distance outside the native
gap brackets $3.48\times10^{-10}$ hartree and largest Pfaffian
solve residual $2.36\times10^{-13}$. The comparison tolerance
remains $2\times10^{-8}$ hartree. This native SCF/query/export
run takes 301.50 s; the independent dense analysis takes 4.99 s.
Its sampled process-tree RSS maximum is 18.5 GiB over 296
successful one-second observations. These timings cover different
tasks and must not be interpreted as a factorization speedup.

The 131-step scalar SCF and complete export take 1794.77 s on
one MPI process and two OpenMP threads. The 1751 one-second RSS
observations have no failures and give a maximum process-tree
sum of 16.5 GiB, with the same memory-accounting caveats as above.
The workstation is an Apple M4 Max with 36 GiB RAM; the retained
host record also gives OS and numerical-library versions.
Raw termination inputs, outputs, methods, complete exports,
successful and rejected interval bounds are retained separately
in \path{bismuth-termination-validation/} under the development

\subsection{A physical cross-basis comparison of the finite Bi states}
The 16-atom termination is also calculated with UZH TZVP at the
same positions, cell, 300 K scalar smearing, grid cutoffs and
post-SCF SOC potential. The converged DZVP restart supplies only
an initial scalar guess; all 272 scalar AOs and 544 spinor states
are recomputed. The SCF converges in 119 steps, and the full run
takes 1628.81 s. Its 1588 one-second memory samples have no
failures and give a process-tree RSS maximum of 16.4 GiB.
The electronic gap becomes $0.008515556694$ hartree
(231.7 meV), 5.51\% above DZVP. The occupied and empty frontier
boundary weights are 80.66\% and 77.19\%, compared with 82.62\%
and 79.70\% in DZVP. Such Lowdin weights themselves depend on
the AO basis and are not the basis-convergence metric used below.

To compare actual states across bases, the reference independently
integrates the rectangular physical overlap
$S_{ab}^{\rm DT}=\langle\phi_a^{\rm D}|\phi_b^{\rm T}\rangle$
from the exported Gaussian contractions. No coefficient padding,
matching of individual degenerate eigenvectors or identification
of separate Lowdin coordinate frames is used. For any selected
spinor subspaces, the singular values of
\begin{equation}
 M=C_{\rm D}^{\dagger}\operatorname{diag}(S^{\rm DT},S^{\rm DT})C_{\rm T}
\end{equation}
are their principal-angle cosines. Each frame is first checked in
its own physical metric. Complete occupied manifolds and complete
frontier Kramers pairs are compared. For equal ranks,
$\|P_{\rm D}-P_{\rm T}\|=\sqrt{1-\sigma_{\min}(M)^2}$ and
$\|P_{\rm D}-P_{\rm T}\|_F^2=2r-2\sum_j\sigma_j(M)^2$.
Tests verify invariance under independent unitary frame rotations
and nonsingular AO basis changes, including complex matrices,
and reject non-normalized or nonfinite inputs.

The DZVP AO space is embedded in TZVP using
$E=(S^{\rm TT})^{-1}S^{\rm TD}$. In the DZVP orthonormal metric,
the residual of $S^{\rm DD}-E^\dagger S^{\rm TT}E$ is
$2.86\times10^{-14}$; all three corresponding position-operator
residuals are below $1.17\times10^{-12}$ bohr. This checks the
physical nested basis and its operators, not just matrix dimensions.
The self-consistent Hamiltonians, however, are different: their
projected difference has norm $0.0053733$ hartree. This is a
basis-dependent change of the SCF potential, not an export error.

For the 80-dimensional occupied spaces, the smallest overlap
singular value is 0.99449263, the projector distance is 0.10480657,
and the mean retained weight is 99.6866\%. The corresponding
projector distances for the occupied and empty frontier doublets
are 0.09276768 and 0.06853998. A same-export DZVP control
reproduces its Hamiltonian within $4.51\times10^{-14}$ hartree;
principal-angle sines near $4\times10^{-8}$ in that control
reflect the square-root sensitivity of $1-\sigma^2$ to roundoff.

Of the same 45 normalized position--energy--scale queries, two
are nontrivial in TZVP instead of three in DZVP. At the centroid
and $\kappa=0.003$ hartree/bohr, the lower-quarter neutral-gap
query becomes trivial while the midpoint and upper-quarter
queries remain nontrivial. The neutral midpoint itself changes
with basis, so these energies are defined relative to each
calculation's own gap, not held at a common absolute value.
The attempted bound on $[0.00275,0.0035]$ fails after 271
evaluations near $0.002851703$ hartree/bohr and is retained as a
negative result. The smaller interval $[0.003,0.0035]$ is
resolved with $\nu=1$, norm constant 16.04838 bohr and minimum
lower bound $1.12998\times10^{-4}$ hartree. This interval is
contained in all previously resolved windows, but the shift of
its lower boundary and the cross-basis subspace distances prevent
a claim of complete-basis convergence.

A four-step native TZVP restart evaluates the same 45 queries
and exports its own complete spectrum. All indices match the
independent reconstruction, including the lower-quarter query
that differs from DZVP. The maximum gap distance outside the
native bracket is $5.21\times10^{-10}$ hartree, at the unchanged
$2\times10^{-8}$ tolerance, and the largest Pfaffian solve
residual is $1.08\times10^{-13}$. The full native run takes
486.42 s, compared with 9.49 s for its independent dense analysis;
these include different work and do not measure a solver speedup.
Its 478 successful one-second samples give a process-tree RSS
maximum of 20.4 GiB. The fresh-export electronic gap changes
by only $4.02\times10^{-11}$ hartree from the spectrum run.

\subsection{A separate finite-size control}
Increasing the patch from three to four primitive cells in each
in-plane direction and removing the two singly coordinated corners
gives 30 atoms, 150 electrons, 390 UZH DZVP AOs and 780 spinors.
The 300 K smearing, 400/40 Ry grids, 20 \AA\ vacuum and SOC
potential are unchanged. The bounding cell grows with the bare,
unrelaxed flake. The atomic-guess SCF converges in 132 steps;
SCF and export take 2601.85 s on the same one-rank, two-thread
setup. The 2530 successful one-second samples give a process-tree
RSS maximum of 19.4 GiB, with the qualifications above.

The neutral SOC gap is $0.002650895533$ hartree (72.14 meV),
compared with 219.6 meV for the smaller DZVP patch, and
$g_{\rm el}/(k_BT)=2.7903$. Fourteen of 30 atoms are boundary
atoms, whereas the complete occupied and empty frontier doublets
carry 80.82\% and 71.04\% of their Lowdin weight on the boundary.
The declining electronic gap and strong boundary weights prevent
an interpretation as a converged bulk band gap.

The original 45-query scan has three nontrivial results, all
at the centroid and $\kappa=0.0003$ hartree/bohr. This equals
the smaller DZVP patch's count but at a tenfold smaller scale;
equal counts obscure the physical change. A 20-scale scan at
the neutral midpoint and centroid finds nontrivial samples at
0.00025--0.0004 and 0.0015--0.00275 hartree/bohr, separated by
trivial samples. The coarse grid missed the latter region.

Numerical gap covers confirm two nontrivial intervals rather
than interpolating these sampled indices. On
$[0.00025,0.0004]$ hartree/bohr, 17 midpoint evaluations cover
nine subintervals with minimum lower bound
$1.82336\times10^{-5}$ hartree. On $[0.0015,0.0025]$,
15 evaluations cover eight intervals with minimum lower bound
$7.67239\times10^{-5}$ hartree. Both use the same norm
constant, 22.28339 bohr, and $10^{-10}$ hartree numerical margin.
In contrast, the interval $[0.003,0.0035]$, which is nontrivial
in both 16-atom bases, is entirely trivial for the 30-atom
Hamiltonian: nine evaluations cover five intervals with minimum
lower bound $8.73676\times10^{-5}$ hartree and anchor $\nu=0$.
This is a resolved opposite index, not a failed or unresolved
numerical factorization. These finite-size changes must remain
visible when evaluating possible material convergence.

A separate Tacho C-interface check of all 20 reconstructed
fine-scan skew matrices matches every Pfaffian index, with maximum
solve residual $2.11\times10^{-12}$. This tests factorization,
not native AO assembly, and does not replace the following check.

The separate five-step native restart evaluates all 45 original
queries and exports a fresh complete spectrum. Every native
index matches its independently reconstructed counterpart, with
maximum distance outside the native gap bracket
$1.31\times10^{-10}$ hartree and largest Pfaffian solve
residual $2.02\times10^{-13}$. The comparison tolerance remains
$2\times10^{-8}$ hartree. The full native run takes 1169.99 s;
the separate dense reconstruction and analysis take 24.31 s.
The 1147 successful one-second samples give a process-tree RSS
maximum of 18.2 GiB, not a factorization-only memory measurement.
The spectrum-run and native-run electronic gaps differ by
$3.62\times10^{-11}$ hartree.

Cases, original methods, positive and negative bounds, cross-basis
comparisons and tests are retained under \path{validation/development/}
in \path{bismuth-size-basis-validation/}, without replacing the

Archive-only replay verifies 270 members in six archives and

and all reported accepted or rejected interval bounds without

\section{Independent Wilson and TRIM Eigenframe Symmetry}
\label{sec:si-property-symmetry}
This validation concerns an independent post-SCF property point set,
not reuse of SCF quadrature weights. The default full-diagonalization
route is compared with the optional K290 and SPGLIB property backends.
All calculations retain the original ordered links and reciprocal
boundary shifts. The new regression inputs use MOLOPT UZH bases;
SOC cases use GTH SOC pseudopotentials.

The scalar He fixture has one atom at fractional coordinates
$(1/2,1/2,1/2)$ in a cubic 4 \AA{} cell. Its four-point directed
NNKP loop includes an explicit reciprocal-cell closing shift.
Both backends use three scalar diagonalizations and recover the
full-loop Berry phase $\pi$. For Ne/SOC in a cubic 5 \AA{} cell,
the refined $8\times5$ property surface has 40 points, reduced to
15 scalar and 15 SOC diagonalizations. All 13 scalar AO states
are retained in the second variation, and the exported subspace
contains eight occupied spinors. All five Wilson spectra and
40 directed links are compared, not just the trivial final index.
An earlier five-scalar-state window cuts a degenerate unoccupied
group. That input is retained as an expected rejection, rather
than used as a quantitative reference.

The comparison evaluator independently integrates contracted
Cartesian Gaussians with the exported spherical contraction
coefficients. For every point it obtains the physical sewing
matrix $G=C_{\rm full}^\dagger S C_{\rm rec}$ and tests both
frame norms, $G^\dagger G-I$, and the residual subspace norm
of $C_{\rm rec}-C_{\rm full}G$. Every directed MMN connection
is checked against $G_i^\dagger M_{ij}^{\rm full}G_j$.
Wilson centers are compared modulo one with permutation and
branch-cut handling. A fixed $2\times10^{-7}$ threshold is used
for these development comparisons, including energies in hartree;
the Ne and He errors are orders of magnitude smaller. Seven
synthetic sensitivity tests include complex degenerate spinor
gauges in a nonorthogonal metric, missing subspace components,
wrong metrics, wrong link conjugation, branch cuts, and
nonfinite overlap data.

The nontrivial stanene TRIM control uses the geometry of the
earlier parity fixture, two Sn atoms and 26 scalar AO functions.
Its 52-state second-variational problem exports eight occupied
spinors. A Gamma-centered, symmetry-reduced $2\times2\times1$
SCF mesh with 400 Ry cutoff, 100 Ry relative cutoff, and
$10^{-10}$ SCF tolerance gives two property representatives
for the four TRIM. K290 and SPGLIB both retain $\nu=1$.
For SPGLIB, independent comparison with four full
diagonalizations gives a maximum complete-spectrum discrepancy
$3.76\times10^{-13}$ hartree, a physical subspace residual
$1.68\times10^{-13}$, and an MMN covariance residual below
$1.6\times10^{-14}$. TRIM connections in this test are
self-links; they do not establish Wilson-surface convergence.
A separate $4\times4\times1$, 1200/100 Ry control gives
complete-spectrum discrepancies below $3.2\times10^{-13}$
hartree for both backends.

The same 400/100 Ry frozen-potential setup also tests nontrivial
Wilson connections. An initial $8\times5$ surface with one allowed
refinement reaches 144 points and nine loops, with 30 representatives.
Full diagonalization, K290 and SPGLIB all report insufficient Wilson
sampling convergence. Nevertheless, the exported frames and connections
agree: the SPGLIB full-spectrum error is below $3.5\times10^{-13}$
hartree, the physical subspace residual below $3.1\times10^{-13}$,
and the directed-link covariance residual below $2.2\times10^{-13}$.
This validates reconstruction, not the convergence of that coarse surface.

A second calculation starts with 12 points per loop and 13 transverse
loops and permits four refinements. All three routes terminate normally
with $\nu=1$ at 192 points per loop and 193 loops, or 37,056 points.
The successive representative counts are 61/156, 217/600, 817/2352,
3169/9312 and 12,481/37,056 for both backends. The final comparison
checks every occupied eigenvalue, every Wilson spectrum modulo one,
and the singular spectra of all 37,056 directed MMN links.
For K290 and SPGLIB, respectively, the maximum occupied-energy errors
are $8.50\times10^{-13}$ and $2.05\times10^{-12}$ eV, the Wilson-center
errors are $2.21\times10^{-13}$ and $2.10\times10^{-13}$, and the
all-link singular-value errors are $4.92\times10^{-13}$ and
$4.27\times10^{-13}$. This large run omits the full AO-state export;
its external comparison is gauge-invariant, whereas the smaller
144-point run additionally verifies physical subspaces and the full
gauge-covariant link matrices. Native H/S and SOC residual checks
remain active in both runs.

These are correctness measurements, not a demonstrated runtime gain.
Single complete runs, including SCF and all refinements, take
226.1 s without property reduction, 254.3 s with K290 and 252.5 s
with SPGLIB on an Apple M4 Max using two MPI ranks and two OpenMP
threads per rank, with one BLAS thread. In this small 26-AO problem,
orbit planning, AO-frame reconstruction and target-operator checks
offset the saved scalar and SOC diagonalizations; cross-k integrals
and links are still built explicitly. Repeated timings, larger bases
and distributed property representatives would be needed for a
scaling claim.

The rejected controls are significant. The original shifted,
unreduced $4\times4\times1$ SCF mesh at 200/40 Ry breaks
threefold equivalence: the maximum spectral splitting among
the nominally equivalent M points is
$1.06127\times10^{-3}$ hartree. Both reconstruction backends
reject its frozen H/S problem. Symmetry-preserving SCF sampling
alone is insufficient: at 600/40 Ry an independent comparison
still found $2.86206\times10^{-7}$ hartree error in the full
spectrum. This motivated the additional projected-H residual
check. Raising the relative integration cutoff, rather than
relaxing an acceptance threshold, eliminates that discrepancy
in the tested controls. These are operator-consistency checks,
not a complete-basis or physical material-convergence claim.

Ordinary incomplete \texttt{SCHEME GENERAL} integration stars
remain invalid. Separate negative runs check this for K290 and
SPGLIB, the scalar-window degeneracy boundary for both backends,
and an invalid operator tolerance. The property-only planner
may handle incomplete stars, but cannot change the SCF closure
requirement. A multi-atom test also exercises complete mirrored
image-cell storage for the dense Fourier H/S check; the sparse
source retains its original triangular DBCSR storage convention.

The final native regression driver passes 220 checks in each of the
SSMP and two-rank PSMP runs, spanning the new 12-input directory and
the existing topology, little-group and Kubo directories. Two OpenMP
threads and one BLAS thread are used, with an explicit 64 MiB OpenMP
stack. An earlier serial attempt using the runtime-default stack
timed out during pre-property grid collocation and was stopped; its
log and stack sample are retained and are not counted as a successful
run. No numerical reference or operator tolerance was changed for
the completed repeat.

The automatic TQC smoke uses two Ne atoms, 16 occupied spinors, 56
sampled points and 82 directed compatibility segments. All three
routes remain compatible and preserve the sampled atomic signature.
This particular generated graph already has 56 independent property
points, so it tests interface compatibility rather than additional
eigenproblem reduction. The final build also repeats the
symmetry-breaking stanene control: full diagonalization completes,
whereas both reduced routes reject the fourth point with
AO-scaled H residual $1.2923\times10^{-5}$ and projected-H residual
$2.8702\times10^{-4}$. The metric residual is only
$1.69\times10^{-14}$, demonstrating why normalization alone is
insufficient.

Inputs, final native sources, build settings, rejected controls and
comparison methods are retained in
\nolinkurl{validation/development/property-symmetry/}.
Archive-only replay verifies 394 file entries in 28 archives and
passes all 23 quantitative comparisons and seven sensitivity tests.
The frame comparisons include direct SSMP--PSMP full-diagonalization
controls as well as both reconstruction backends. Across these
comparisons, the largest full-spectrum discrepancy is
$8.24\times10^{-13}$ hartree, the largest physical subspace residual
$3.08\times10^{-13}$, and the largest directed-link covariance
residual $2.12\times10^{-13}$. The two large-surface comparisons use
the separate gauge-invariant criteria described above.
This replay reruns the archived comparison algorithms, not the native
DFT calculations. Final shared-library checksums are recorded along
with executable checksums; the earlier exploratory runs retain their
original, executable-only provenance.

\subsection{Independent scalar MPI groups}
A subsequent validation distributes the independent scalar
diagonalizations over CP2K's existing k-point MPI groups.
The tested group sizes are automatic, all ranks in one group,
and two ranks per group, with two and four total MPI ranks and
two OpenMP threads per rank. Both K290 and SPGLIB are compared
with full diagonalization for He, Ne/SOC and stanene/SOC.
The He case deliberately distributes three representatives over
two two-rank groups. The final Ne mesh distributes 15
representatives over four one-rank groups. These unequal workloads
test local indexing and transfer from the owning group, rather
than only the evenly divisible case. Native checks require exactly
one owning group for each representative.

All 24 physical frame/link comparisons pass. Across the matrix,
the maximum complete-spectrum error is $7.23\times10^{-13}$ hartree,
the maximum physical subspace residual $1.69\times10^{-13}$,
and the maximum directed-link covariance residual
$1.94\times10^{-14}$. The largest Wilson-center difference is
$8.89\times10^{-16}$ in reduced units. Four invalid group requests
are rejected, and four additional four-rank runs reject a scalar
SOC window ending inside a degenerate group, for both backends
and both one- and two-rank groups. The latter checks that the
additional boundary eigenvalue is communicated consistently.
There are 35 native cases in total, including these expected
failures. The regular 13-input property directory also passes
27 checks in SSMP and 27 with four-rank PSMP.

The records in \nolinkurl{validation/development/property-groups/}
include native inputs and outputs, source and shared-library
provenance, grouping diagnostics, and the independent comparison
programs. Archive replay repeats the 24 physical comparisons
without a DFT rerun. This validates parallel correctness, not
multi-node performance. In particular, SOC assembly, reconstruction
checks, and directed links are not yet distributed across the
independent k-point groups. The earlier 37,056-point timings
above belong to the preceding single-group implementation.

\section{Submission Validation Still Required}
The current record does not establish a nontrivial, size-converged
DFT localizer classification for a large SOC flake, multi-node sparse
scaling, or general 230-space-group TQC.
Further SOC systems and converged flakes require basis/cutoff
controls, spatial/energy/scale scans, independent band references,
timings and whole-process memory. DZVP/TZVP is not a complete-basis
extrapolation, and development diagnostics or plans must not be
reported as completed material validation.
