\section{Scope and Reproduction Boundaries}
This working document combines the retained band-topology calculations
transferred from the k-point manuscript with separately validated
transport, localizer, and quadratic-pseudospectrum developments.
The original results are not recomputed or relabelled by moving
the manuscript text.
The development status is recorded as of 24 September 2026.
A source freeze and complete public reproduction archive remain
submission requirements.

\subsection*{Applications versus test calculations}
TopoHSE-DB and the submitted generative--predictive discovery study
are cited in the main text as materials-science applications.
Their VASP-based calculations are not part of the CP2K validation
records collected here. In particular, their database labels and
candidate counts do not validate the new CP2K localizer or transport
implementations.

The smaller calculations below are tests and demonstrations.
The companion k-point paper additionally reports 18 converged Si/Al
calculations in conventional cubic cells with eight Si atoms
($a=5.43$~\AA) and four Al atoms ($a=4.05$~\AA).
These use PBE, DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH bases, GTH-PBE pseudopotentials,
600/60~Ry grid cutoffs, and an SCF threshold of $10^{-9}$.
Al uses Fermi--Dirac occupations at 1000~K, so its compared quantity
is the electronic free energy. The same-mesh comparison uses
$4\times4\times4$ sampling: 64 full-grid points, 32 with time reversal,
and four with either K290 or SPGLIB atomic symmetry.
The maximum cell-energy/free-energy difference is
$1.4\times10^{-12}$ hartree; the Al atomic-symmetry stress difference
is 0.098 bar. These tests demonstrate representation consistency,
not independently converged material properties or general force accuracy.
Their source and raw records remain those of the companion manuscript;
no new Si/Al calculations were performed for the present discussion.

\section{Native Wilson-Loop Test and Demonstration Calculations}

The topology examples form a separate calculation set from the Si/Al
mainline tests. The native extension supplies directed Gaussian-basis
overlaps, second-variational spinors, and the Wilson-loop analysis described
in the main text. The retained calculations were completed on
17 September 2026 with one MPI process and one OpenMP thread. Source
provenance and artifact checksums are recorded in
\texttt{validation/topology/README.md} and the figure manifest.

Both examples use PBE, the \texttt{DZVP-MOLOPT-GGA-GTH} basis from
\texttt{BASIS\_MOLOPT\_UZH}, SOC pseudopotentials from
\texttt{GTH\_SOC\_POTENTIALS}, a 200 Ry density cutoff and a 40 Ry relative
cutoff. The potential labels are \texttt{GTH-PBE-q8} for neon and
\texttt{GTH-PBE-q4} for tin. Complex orbitals and explicit full SCF meshes
are used. The neon reference contains one atom at fractional coordinate
$(1/2,1/2,1/2)$ in a cubic cell of side 5~\AA, with a $2\times2\times2$
SCF mesh and convergence threshold $10^{-9}$. The stanene cell has lattice
vectors $(4.674,0,0)$, $(2.337,4.047802737288466,0)$, and $(0,0,20)$~\AA,
and two tin atoms at fractional positions $(1/3,1/3,0.5213)$ and
$(2/3,2/3,0.4787)$. It therefore has a buckling height of 0.852~\AA.
These fixed geometric parameters follow the pristine-stanene reference
reported by Zhang et al.\footnote{R.-w. Zhang et al., Room temperature
quantum spin Hall insulator in ethynyl-derivative functionalized stanene
films, \emph{Sci. Rep.} \textbf{6}, 18879 (2016),
\url{https://doi.org/10.1038/srep18879}.}
An $8\times8\times1$ SCF mesh and convergence threshold $10^{-8}$ are used
for stanene. The examples test the topology implementation at these fixed
settings, rather than constitute a new structural or band-gap prediction.

Eight occupied spinors are retained in each case. The stanene
second-variational calculation includes all 26 scalar basis states, giving
52 spinor states before occupied-band selection. For neon, five scalar
states produce ten spinors. The selected half-plane is
$\mathbf k(s,t)=(t,s/2,0)$ in fractional reciprocal coordinates, with
$0\leq s,t\leq1$. The loop closes with reciprocal shift $(1,0,0)$.
Starting meshes contain four points on each of three loops for neon and
12 points on each of 13 loops for stanene. Refinement doubles the points
per loop and inserts a midpoint between neighboring transverse loops.
The accepted surfaces have $8\times5$ and $192\times193$ points,
respectively. The last center changes are $1.28\times10^{-5}$ and
$2.73\times10^{-4}$ in reduced units. The smallest link singular values
are 0.99826 and 0.95248.

The native invariants are zero for neon and one for stanene. Z2Pack 2.2.1
gives the same stanene parity using either the raw overlaps or their
unitary polar factors. For the latter, the maximum Wilson-eigenvalue
difference from the native calculation is $7.95\times10^{-14}$. This
comparison tests the loop algebra with identical input matrices. Separate
operator checks enforce zero-step normalization, reverse-link adjoint
relations, loop reversal, and atomic-position phases. A bonded two-atom
full-spinor check gives maximum adjoint and zero-step residuals of
$2.79\times10^{-14}$ and $2.00\times10^{-13}$, respectively.

An additional adaptive Z2Pack-driven stanene surface uses 42 CP2K line
calculations and ends with 15 accepted transverse lines, all satisfying
the line-position, movement, and gap checks, and again gives $\nu=1$.
This calculation chooses its sampling independently but uses the same
CP2K Hamiltonian and overlap implementation. The main-text figure shows
the native samples without interpolation or artificial separation of
degenerate centers. It is regenerated by
\texttt{python3 validation/topology/plot\_wilson.py}, which requires NumPy
and Matplotlib. Raw \texttt{.wilson} files, inputs, logs, comparison
reports, generated comma-separated figure data, and checksums accompany
the figure.

\subsection{Parameter-mesh discretization and physical closure}
Let $Q_\mu(x)$ be the polar-unitary selected-state overlap directed from
mesh vertex $x$ to $x+\hat\mu$. All boundary links include the specified
physical sewing. The oriented plaquette is
\begin{equation}
 P_{\mu\nu}(x)=Q_\mu(x)Q_\nu(x+\hat\mu)
 Q_\mu^\dagger(x+\hat\nu)Q_\nu^\dagger(x).
\end{equation}
With this link orientation, the two-parameter estimator is
\begin{equation}
 C_1^{\mathrm{mesh}}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_x
 \operatorname{Arg}\det P_{12}(x).
\end{equation}
Its sign matches the determinant Wilson-phase winding convention of the
main text. For four parameters, define the full principal matrix
logarithm $F_{\mu\nu}(x)=\log P_{\mu\nu}(x)$. Each $F_{\mu\nu}$ is
anti-Hermitian and already includes the plaquette area. The estimator is
\begin{equation}
 C_2^{\mathrm{mesh}}=-\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\sum_x
 \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}
 \left[F_{12}F_{34}-F_{13}F_{24}+F_{14}F_{23}\right](x).
 \label{eq:si-c2}
\end{equation}
The full matrices are needed for a non-Abelian occupied subspace.
Equation~\eqref{eq:si-c2} approximates the second Chern character pairing
and is not generally the second Chern class. Its finite-mesh value is
reported without integer rounding.

Physical links use $C_a^\dagger O_{ab}C_b$, with both spinor components
summed when present. The cross-geometry AO matrix $O_{ab}$ retains the
moving contracted Gaussian centers, periodic images, and reciprocal
boundary phases. The cell, atom count and selected rank remain fixed
throughout the family. Endpoint checks use explicit atom permutations,
lattice translations, basis matching, and selected-subspace sewing.
The code reports AO-metric errors, sampled selected/excluded-band gaps,
minimum link singular values, and principal plaquette phases. A refined
mesh must preserve the same physical family. Sampled isolation and
invariant convergence do not by themselves establish a bulk spectral
gap between samples. An indirect gap is interpreted only for a lowest-band
prefix with a justified common energy reference across geometries.

\section{Native Inversion Representations and Symmetry Signatures}
\label{sec:si-inversion}

The inversion extension was validated separately from the phason
extension and from the earlier Si/Al and Wilson calculations.
Its source patch, base snapshot, inputs and outputs remain in
\texttt{validation/topology/inversion-tqc/}.
These records identify the original tests without retrospectively
assigning them to another executable.

The \texttt{WANNIER90} print section accepts
\texttt{KPOINTS\_SOURCE TRIM}, \texttt{SOC T},
\texttt{TIME\_REVERSAL T} and \texttt{INVERSION\_TQC T}.
\texttt{TQC\_DIMENSION} selects the four $k_z=0$ TRIM or all eight
three-dimensional TRIM. \texttt{PARITY\_ORIGIN} specifies the inversion
center in fractional direct coordinates. The occupied spinor bands must
be a complete lowest-band prefix, with excluded states available for
checking spectral separation. A \texttt{PARITY}-only mode also accepts
isolated scalar subspaces. Neither route requires Z2Pack, Wannier90
execution or a Wannier fit. The TRIM calculation holds the converged
SCF potential fixed and does not require the SCF integration mesh to
contain those points. The two-dimensional option samples the fractional
$k_z=0$ plane of a three-dimensionally periodic cell, not a separate
two-dimensional Poisson boundary condition. The inversion center is
specified explicitly rather than inferred from a space-group search.

For an orbital of angular momentum $l$ on atom $a$, inversion about
$\mathbf c$ maps it to an identical Gaussian orbital on atom $b$ when
$2\mathbf c-\mathbf r_a=\mathbf r_b+\mathbf L$ with integer $\mathbf L$.
The coefficient multiplier is
$(-1)^l\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf L)$. The code verifies
atom-kind matching, involution, metric covariance, $C^\dagger SC=I$,
unitarity and Hermiticity of the projected inversion matrix, and its
commutator with the selected eigenvalues. Spinful time reversal is
checked through the sewing matrix of $\Theta=\mathrm{i}\sigma_y K$.
Parity multiplicities must be even before division into Kramers pairs.
The dimensionless and geometry tolerances are $10^{-6}$ and
$10^{-6}$ bohr, respectively. The energy-commutator tolerance is
$10^{-6}$ hartree.

For $N$ occupied Kramers pairs and odd counts $n^-_\kappa$ at
$\mathbf k=\kappa/2$, $\kappa\in\{0,1\}^d$, the parity index is
$\nu=\sum_\kappa n^-_\kappa\bmod2$. In three dimensions the weak indices
restrict this sum to $\kappa_i=1$, and the convention for the inversion
indicator is $z_4=\sum_\kappa n^-_\kappa\bmod4$.
The analytic inversion-subgroup EBRs are local even or odd Kramers pairs
at centers $\mathbf c+\mathbf a/2$. Define
\begin{equation}
 d_{\mathbf a}=2^{-d}\sum_\kappa(-1)^{\mathbf a\cdot\kappa}
                     (N-2n^-_\kappa).
\end{equation}
An integer atomic-signature decomposition exists when all
$d_{\mathbf a}$ are integers and $N-\sum_{\mathbf a}d_{\mathbf a}$ is even.
A nonnegative decomposition additionally requires
$N\geq\sum_{\mathbf a}|d_{\mathbf a}|$. The implementation makes these
decisions with integer arithmetic. Atomic compatibility concerns the
symmetry signature, not a proof of trivial topology. Likewise, a
signed-only decomposition does not by itself establish fragile topology.
This inversion-only EBR test is distinct from the subsequent native
little-group and explicit-segment analysis described below; it does
not become a general EBR classifier when that additional analysis is used.

\section{Native Little-Group and Compatibility Validation}
\label{sec:si-little-groups}
The native algebra constructs unitary projective character tables,
Wigner corepresentation restrictions, and endpoint-to-segment
branching without a runtime dependency on Spgrep or a pretabulated
irrep catalogue. The electronic-structure path uses SPGLIB to detect
spatial operations and, when requested, adds their nonmagnetic
time-reversal extension. It retains centering translations and tests
the metric, band-subspace closure, energy commutators and the complete
semilinear multiplication law before accepting band multiplicities.

The all-setting sweep covers all 530 Hall settings of the 230 ordinary
space groups. At each setting, it samples
$(0,0,0)$, $(1/2,1/2,1/2)$, $(1/2,0,0)$, $(1/3,1/3,0)$,
$(0,0,1/4)$ and $(0.173,0.217,0.319)$ with scalar and spinful factors.
The invariant cell metric is obtained by averaging over the supplied
rotations. Both the ordinary group and its grey extension are checked.
Generic-site orbits with physical spin multiplicity provide fixed-rank
compatible band data. Segments run from Gamma to the other sampled
points and from Gamma to its reciprocal image $(0,0,1)$.
This tests directed-image monodromy as well as ordinary endpoint
restrictions; it does not sample every possible line or material.

Analytic cases separately test magnetic half translations, spin

reciprocal-image monodromy and changed coset representatives are also

The Gaussian directory uses UZH MOLOPT bases for its GPW fixtures and
a contracted all-electron basis for GAPW. Ne tests exercise scalar
and post-SCF GTH-SOC multiplets at Gamma, a generic PT-symmetric
point and nonsymmorphic screw sectors. Scalar He/GPW, He/GAPW and
Ne/SOC close a four-segment reciprocal loop; a separately selected
single screw branch must report its monodromy obstruction.
The generated Wilson-link test also exercises repeated refinement.
Serial runs use two OpenMP threads; MPI runs use two ranks with two
threads each and the thread-safe BLAS settings stated below.
An earlier wider corepresentation integration pass additionally gave
110/110 serial and 111/111 MPI assertions across topology, Kubo,
localizer and quadratic directories. These are separate test scopes,
not numbers to add into a material benchmark total.

A separate local algebra sweep uses SPGLIB 2.7 magnetic data in the
supplied setting for every one of the 1,651 magnetic UNI numbers.
At the same six points and two spin choices, all 19,812 factor cases
pass; 14,088 contain an antiunitary stabilizer, and 5,724 do not.
For 10,154 primitive-cell tables supported by an independent
Spgrep 0.7.0 comparison, dimensions, Wigner types and unitary
restriction characters agree within $7.11\times10^{-15}$.
Both calculations use the same supplied factor system, so this is
not an independent validation of symmetry detection. Moreover, a
separate semilinear-product check did not validate every explicit
antiunitary matrix produced by that reference calculation.
Those matrices are not imported into CP2K; the comparison is limited
to types, dimensions and unitary restrictions. CP2K's actual Gaussian
sewing matrices pass their own full product check.

The separate native site-induction test samples fractional positions
$(0,0,0)$, $(1/2,0,1/4)$ and $(0.173,0.217,0.319)$ at the same six
reciprocal points. Ordinary and grey groups, each with scalar and
spinful factors, give 38,160 site/k-point cases over all 530 Hall
settings. There are 67,824 local-representation columns evaluated and
decomposed, and the same number of directed compatibility comparisons.
Their atomic band rank equals the orbit multiplicity times the local
irrep or corepresentation dimension. This construction preserves
conventional-cell translation sectors rather than silently converting
them to a primitive-cell catalogue.

An independent check constructs Cartesian s/p/d orbital traces and,
for spinors, multiplies them by the physical SU(2) trace. After matching
the onsite orbital content, its explicitly translated orbit trace
agrees with the corresponding induced character sum in 114,480
comparisons. The numerical acceptance tolerance is $10^{-7}$ for
these summed traces and decomposition checks; group and orbit geometry
use $10^{-9}$. Analytic checks additionally cover inversion-center
parities, screw orbits, spin-quaternion doublets, and complex local
orbitals related by an antiunitary half translation. These are
algorithmic reference tests, not new material calculations or a new
production input mode.
Additional scalar and spinful checks preserve the characters under
passive origin shifts and unimodular changes to a sheared cell, and
verify the expected Bloch rephasing under integer changes of operation
representatives. The full database sweeps pass in serial, two-rank MPI,
and a separate bounds-checked build with floating-point traps.

The native Wyckoff generator separately constructs 3,467 affine
site-symmetry families across all 530 Hall settings and determines
5,648 directed specialization relations. External validation uses
SPGLIB's tabulated \texttt{Wyckoff.csv} coordinates, not the production
fixed-set algorithm, to check dimensions, orbit multiplicities,
family equivalence and closure containment. Every family matches
bijectively, with largest affine residual $2.22\times10^{-16}$.
The reference table's version and checksum, comparison script and
results are retained in the development record. No tabulated
coordinates are read by the CP2K implementation.

For each generated seed the separate spinful induction kernel
confirms its orbit and site stabilizer. Combined arbitrary origin
shifts, nonorthogonal unimodular cell changes, integer shifts of
operation representatives and reordered operations preserve the
families and their specialization graph. Analytic tests cover
inversion centers, intersecting mirrors, fixed-point-free screw
axes, grey groups and an antiunitary half translation. Failure to
resolve a generic stabilizer or an arithmetic/work limit returns
no partial list. Maximal site groups are identified geometrically;
this is not an elementarity test for their induced representations.
The complete Wyckoff sweep passes in serial release, two-rank MPI
and bounds-checked debug builds.

The subsequent atomic-signature module combines all generated sites
and onsite irreps/corepresentations over the six reciprocal points
listed above. Ordinary/grey and scalar/spinful variants yield 2,120
matrices, 31,079 provenance-preserving columns and 707,038 integer
entries. Seven directed comparisons per column include the supplied
point connections and a reciprocal-image loop, totalling 217,553
column/segment checks. Each block reproduces the column's band rank;
antiunitary rows count full corepresentations rather than treating all
states as Kramers pairs. Additivity, permuted operation rows, and
integer coset rephasing are checked. All sixteen inversion-center
signatures and the generic-site signature are recovered analytically.

These full sweeps pass in serial release, two-rank MPI and debug with
bounds and floating-point checks. A local optimized-compiler issue in automatic
\texttt{MATMUL} result resizing was independently reproduced without
CP2K and avoided by explicitly dimensioning the test vectors; no
physical reference values were adjusted. The compiler reproducer and
validation logs are retained separately from material results.
No new general-classification input mode, elementarity assertion or
complete reciprocal-stratum coverage follows from these matrix tests.

The integer-signature extension factors these same 2,120 matrices
using checked 64-bit Smith reduction. Each factorization verifies its
full integer certificate. One signed target per matrix is reconstructed,
and the nonnegative search recovers every atomic column and a
three-column sum per matrix: 33,199 witnesses in total. These deliberately
constructed targets test recovery, not a classification of DFT bands.

An independent unit program compares Smith invariants with the gcds
of all minors and computes the determinants of both unimodular
transforms for 384 matrices with dimensions one through four.
The rank-zero through rank-four case counts are 38, 167, 103, 60 and 16.
The analytic inversion Fourier criterion supplies a separate exact
oracle for every odd-parity-count pattern with two Kramers pairs:
among 81 four-TRIM patterns, 41 admit signed and 33 admit nonnegative
atomic signatures; among 6,561 eight-TRIM patterns, the counts are
241 and 129. Thus signed-only cases are tested explicitly.
For every $2\times3$ matrix with entries in $\{0,1,2\}$ and every
two-component target in $\{0,\ldots,4\}^2$, a brute-force enumeration
of coefficients zero through four supplies another oracle, covering
18,225 systems. Coefficients beyond four cannot contribute to a
target in this range through any nonzero nonnegative column.
Zero columns, deficient rank, invalid data, unsafe arithmetic and
exhausted node budgets have distinct tests. The latter return an

The expanded matrix tests pass in optimized serial and two-rank MPI

MPI repeats the native algebra on each rank and does not
distribute the catalogue construction. The analytic matrix path also

against the existing library; this is not a full no-SPGLIB rebuild.

The Gaussian connection is enabled by \texttt{ATOMIC\_SIGNATURES}.
It compares only physically validated, sampled band subspaces with
the generated reference matrix, preserving conventional-cell sectors.
Band characters are rephased for integer changes of operation
representatives, and the entire product/factor correspondence is
checked, including antiunitary conjugation. All 2,120 algebra cases
recover the same signature after operation and irrep permutations

The subsequent reciprocal-geometry test enumerates fixed families of
$(-1)^aW^{-T}$ for all 530 Hall settings and their grey extensions.

fixed-torus closures, 57,357 specialization incidences and 18,730
